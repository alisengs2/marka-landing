# MARKA London — Editorial Guide

For everyone writing, curating or commissioning content for MARKA London: the
Capsule Edition at the Design Museum on 13 October 2026. `README.md` covers the
code. This file covers the argument — what the day is about, why it is happening
here, how the speakers connect, and how the words and images should read.

---

## 1. The premise

MARKA has run in Istanbul for more than 25 years as an ideas platform across
design, creativity, innovation, business and culture. London is the first edition
outside it — deliberately small, one day, one room, a curated audience rather than
an open conference.

The subject, in the site's own words, is *"the ideas, people and cultural shifts
influencing the way we design, live, choose, consume and experience contemporary
luxury."*

The working thesis behind the programme — the thing every session should be
arguing about from a different side:

> **Luxury has moved from possession to experience.** What is scarce now is
> character, craft, and places and objects that could exist nowhere else. The
> people who decide what matters next are no longer only the ones who make
> things; they are the ones who frame, edit, stage and host them.

If a talk, a bio or a piece of copy cannot be traced back to that sentence, it
probably belongs at a different event.

---

## 2. Why London

London is the argument, not the backdrop. Three reasons to keep returning to,
in copy and on stage:

- **It is where the partnership began.** STEPEVI started its international
  journey in London in 2004. MARKA's own international journey starting in the
  same city is the premise of the partnership page, not a coincidence to be
  mentioned once.
- **It is a city that reuses itself.** Almost every speaker's London work is a
  second life for an existing building: a former institute, a railway hotel, a
  fire station, a Victorian dining room. That is the city's design method, and
  it is also the venue's story (§3).
- **It is an editing capital.** The magazines, museums, studios and dining rooms
  that decide what the rest of the world notices are concentrated here. Half the
  line-up does that editing for a living.

Avoid: London as a shopping list of neighbourhoods and landmarks. Prefer London
as a set of working methods.

## 3. The venue

The Design Museum, Kensington High Street, on the edge of Holland Park. The
conference is in the **Sachs Family Park Room**, second floor.

The building is the strongest single piece of editorial material available:

- Built 1962 as the **Commonwealth Institute** (Robert Matthew, Johnson-Marshall
  and Partners), and kept for its copper hyperbolic-paraboloid roof, which spans
  the full width and lifts at two opposing corners.
- Redesigned inside by **John Pawson**, who calls it *a tent in the park*.
- So: an obsolete institution of one era, re-inhabited as the country's museum of
  contemporary design. The building performs the day's theme before anyone speaks.

Use "a tent in the park" sparingly — once per piece of copy, as Pawson's line,
not as house voice.

The museum itself was founded out of the **Conran Foundation**, which is a live
connection to the line-up rather than trivia (§4).

---

## 4. The partner

STEPEVI is **Exclusive Global Partner**, and the language matters: *intellectual
and cultural partner*, not sponsor, not host. What that means editorially:

- STEPEVI contributes an ecosystem — clients, designers, architects,
  collaborators — into the conversation and the room.
- The shared ground with MARKA is design, craftsmanship, sustainability,
  innovation and contemporary luxury. Those five words are the overlap; brand
  claims outside them should not appear in MARKA copy.
- The partnership is built to travel. London is edition one, not a one-off.

Never write STEPEVI into a speaker's bio or a session description as a placement.
The partnership has its own page and its own voice; the programme stays
editorially independent, and it reads as more credible when it does.

## 5. The line-up, and how it holds together

Order on the site is editorial and set by MARKA. As of this writing:

Es Devlin · Sebastian Conran · Alex Eagle · Jeremy King · Harry Handelsman ·
Dylan Jones · Alfhild Külper · Tony Chambers · Tara Bernerd

They are not nine separate interviews. They are four arguments:

**a. Making worlds — scale, staging, spectacle**
*Es Devlin, Tara Bernerd.* Both build environments people move through and feel
before they analyse. Devlin at the scale of stadium and museum; Bernerd at the
scale of the room you sleep, eat and meet in. The shared question: what is an
audience, and what do you owe it?

**b. Making things — invention, craft, material**
*Sebastian Conran, Alfhild Külper.* Design as objects that work: 2,500 products
and robotics on one side, wool, surplus yarn and the human hand on the other.
Between them sits the whole argument about sustainability, industry and touch.
The shared question: what is worth manufacturing now?

**c. Making places — hospitality, reuse, the city**
*Jeremy King, Harry Handelsman, Alex Eagle.* Dining rooms as cultural landmarks;
buildings given second lives; retail rethought as a way of living. All three
work in London, all three take existing fabric and change what it is for. The
shared question: how do you make somewhere people want to belong to?

**d. Framing it all — editing, taste, influence**
*Dylan Jones, Tony Chambers, Alex Eagle.* Editors and tastemakers: the people who
decide what gets seen, and in what order. The shared question: who confers
value now, and on what authority?

Alex Eagle sits in both **c** and **d** on purpose. That double position is the
most useful hinge in the programme.

### Connections worth building a session on

- **Devlin × the venue.** Her first European retrospective, *Es Devlin: Other
  Worlds*, is at the Design Museum from October 2026. The conference is in the
  same building in the same month. Confirm dates with the museum, then use it —
  it is the single strongest venue–speaker link available.
- **Conran × the venue.** A founding trustee of the Design Museum and Chair of
  the Conran Foundation, speaking in the museum his family's foundation created.
- **King × Handelsman.** Simpson's in the Strand and St Pancras: two revivals of
  London institutions that had stopped working. A conversation about adaptive
  reuse that is really about nerve.
- **Jones × Chambers.** Two editors who shaped British cultural publishing from
  different chairs — and both now working outside the magazine. Do not put them
  on a nostalgia panel; the subject is what replaced the magazine's authority.
- **Devlin × Conran.** Both in the Homo Faber 2026 orbit in Venice; craft and
  imagination from opposite ends of the same question.
- **Bernerd × King.** Interiors and hospitality: who the room is really for.
- **Külper × Conran.** Material intelligence versus industrial invention.

### Cautions

- Two speakers carry the Conran and Wallpaper legacies. Neither should be
  introduced primarily as an inheritance; both bios on the site take care to move
  past that in the first sentence. Keep that.
- Avoid stacking all four women, or all three Londoners, into one session
  because of who they are rather than what they argue.

## 6. House voice

The site's copy is calm, declarative and specific. Match it.

- **British English.** Curly apostrophes and quotes as HTML entities
  (`&rsquo;`, `&ldquo;`, `&rdquo;`), `&middot;` between role fragments,
  `&hellip;`, `&mdash;` used sparingly.
- **Present tense** for what someone does; past tense only for named
  achievements.
- **Concrete over adjectival.** "Le Caprice, The Ivy, The Wolseley" beats "iconic
  restaurants". Name the building, the award, the year.
- **No sales register.** No "unmissable", "world-class", "curated experience" as
  filler, no exclamation marks, no second person in editorial copy.
- **Titles of works in italics** (`<em>`) — exhibitions, books, magazines.
  Platforms, studios and companies stay upright.
- **Luxury** is used as the subject of an argument, never as a compliment.

### Speaker entry pattern

Each entry on `speakers.html` is one `<article class="speaker">` with:

- **Name** — as the person uses it.
- **Role line** — the `.label` treatment, fragments separated by `&middot;`,
  roughly 3–9 words. It is a position, not a tagline.
- **Bio** — one to three paragraphs of about 60–120 words each, in
  `<p class="speaker-bio">`. First paragraph: what they do and why it matters.
  Middle: the work, named. Last: the conviction behind it.
- **Photo credit**, when supplied, as a `<figcaption>` under the image.

Rows alternate left and right automatically, so reordering entries changes which
side a portrait sits on. Nothing else needs touching.

### Portraits

Artwork is delivered as 1080 × 1520 PNG with the red name plate burned in, and
is used whole — never cropped. Convert to JPEG at 852 × 1200 and save as
`assets/speaker-<first>-<last>.jpg`:

```sh
sips -s format jpeg -s formatOptions 82 -z 1200 852 "source.png" \
     --out assets/speaker-first-last.jpg
```

Alt text describes the person and the setting, not the plate: *"Name, role,
photographed in black and white."* Keep `width="852" height="1200"` and
`loading="lazy"` on the `<img>`.

## 7. The audience

Leaders of the luxury industry, creative leaders, cultural tastemakers, and
people shaping design, architecture, fashion, lifestyle, culture and business.
Day Access is £950; the rest of the room is there on STEPEVI's invitation.

Write for someone who already knows the field and came for an argument, not an
introduction. They can be trusted with a proper noun and a difficult question.

Shape of the day: breakfast, morning session, lunch, afternoon session, happy
hour. Roughly four hours of stage time — which is why the programme is four
arguments, not nine monologues.
